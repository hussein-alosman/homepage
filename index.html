Research

My research concerns computational methods for understanding human affect and behaviour from multimodal data, with an emphasis on physiological sensing, machine learning, and human–computer interaction.

I study how emotional and physiological states can be inferred from signals such as EEG, ECG, PPG, facial video and other modalities. A recurring question is how to obtain measurements and models that remain useful across individuals, settings and sensing conditions. More recent work examines multimodal affect recognition in virtual reality and the use and evaluation of language models in mental-health applications.

Research areas

Affective computing and multimodal emotion recognition

Recognition of affect from neural, physiological and visual signals; multimodal fusion; deep representation learning; data augmentation; and generalization across people and contexts.

Related work: EEG and graph neural networks · EEG–facial fusion · PPG generalization

Physiological sensing and measurement

Contactless and wearable approaches to measuring physiological signals, including video-based heart-rate estimation, heart-rate variability, ECG-based biometrics and measurement uncertainty.

Related work: Non-contact heart-rate monitoring · Video HRV · ECG authentication

Machine learning for mental-health applications

Computational analysis of mental-health-related data, including multimodal classification, detection of suicide-related language, synthetic data generation and interpretation of model representations.

Related work: Synthetic data with LLMs · Interpretable risk-factor representations · Bipolar disorder classification

Human–computer interaction and immersive systems

Affect-aware interaction, haptic feedback, biofeedback and immersive environments, including physiological measurement and multimodal datasets in virtual reality.

Related work: WARM-VR · Biofeedback for stress management · Affective haptics
